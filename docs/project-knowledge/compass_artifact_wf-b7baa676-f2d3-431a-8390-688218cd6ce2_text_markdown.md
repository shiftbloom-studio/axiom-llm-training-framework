# The Frontier of LLM Pre-Training: Best Practices and Bleeding-Edge Strategies (Q1–Q2 2026)

## TL;DR
- **Pre-training in 2026 has decisively moved beyond "bigger dense transformers."** The dominant production recipe is now a **hybrid Mamba/linear-attention + Mixture-of-Experts transformer**, trained in **4-bit (NVFP4) precision** with **matrix-orthogonalizing optimizers (Muon/MuonClip)** on **25T+ token corpora** heavy with synthetic and quality-filtered data — exemplified by NVIDIA Nemotron-3, DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K2, and Qwen3.6.
- **The most promising "big leap" candidates are**: (1) diffusion language models as "super data learners" that beat autoregressive models in the data-constrained regime; (2) sub-quadratic hybrid architectures (Mamba-3, Gated DeltaNet-2) that make million-token context economical; (3) native 4-bit pre-training (NVFP4/Quartet) for major compute/memory efficiency; and (4) new pre-training objectives (multi-token prediction, Future Summary Prediction) that inject longer-horizon signal.
- **Sober caveats matter**: rigorous, fairly-tuned benchmarks ("Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers," Stanford) show matrix-based optimizers deliver only ~1.1–1.4× speedups over a well-tuned AdamW (not the advertised 2×), the advantage *shrinks with scale* (1.4× at 0.1B → 1.1× at 1.2B params), and the winner is regime-dependent; many flashy results are at <2B scale and may not extrapolate.

## Key Findings

**1. Architecture: the hybrid Mamba/linear-attention + MoE transformer is the new default.** Nearly every frontier 2026 model interleaves a minority of full-attention layers with a majority of sub-quadratic sequence mixers (Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet) plus sparse MoE feed-forward. Qwen3-Next/Qwen3.5/3.6 use a 3:1 (and later 4:1) Gated DeltaNet-to-attention ratio; NVIDIA Nemotron-3 uses Mamba-2 blocks with periodic attention "anchors"; DeepSeek-V4 ships sparse attention as the default path.

**2. Optimizers: Muon and its stabilized variant MuonClip have crossed into trillion-parameter production.** Kimi K2 (1.04T params) pre-trained on 15.5T tokens with "zero loss spike" using MuonClip; DeepSeek-V4 uses Muon across 32T+ tokens. But fair benchmarks temper the hype.

**3. Low-precision: NVFP4 4-bit pre-training is now demonstrated at the 25T-token, 100B+ parameter scale** (Nemotron-3) — the biggest practical efficiency lever of the year.

**4. Objectives: next-token prediction is being augmented, not replaced**, via multi-token prediction (now standard for speculative decoding) and research objectives like Future Summary Prediction; diffusion LMs are the most radical alternative.

**5. Data: the field has pivoted from quantity to quality, synthetic generation, and dynamic curation** as the web's unique-token supply plateaus.

## Details

### A. Model Architecture

**Hybrid Mamba/linear-attention + transformer is the consensus frontier design.** The core motivation is long-context inference efficiency: full softmax attention is O(n²) compute with a KV cache that grows linearly, while state-space models (Mamba) and linear-attention variants (Gated DeltaNet) maintain a fixed-size recurrent state (O(n) compute, constant memory). The 2026 consensus, validated by systematic studies (e.g., "Hybrid Architectures for Language Models: Systematic Analysis and Design Insights," arXiv 2510.04800), is that pure linear models are weak at recall/state-tracking but a hybrid keeps most of the quality while slashing cost.

- **Qwen3-Next / Qwen3.5 / Qwen3.6 (Alibaba):** Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B combines **Gated DeltaNet + Gated Attention at a 3:1 ratio** (75% of layers linear). Qwen's own ablation found Gated DeltaNet gives "stronger in-context learning ability than commonly used methods like Sliding Window Attention or Mamba2." Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (released April 14, 2026, Apache 2.0) moved to a **4:1 ratio** with Gated Attention on just 10 of 40 layers; reported Terminal-Bench 2.0 up 27% and QwenWebBench up 43% over Qwen3.5. Also features high-sparsity MoE (e.g., Qwen3-Coder-Next: 512 experts, 10 activated, 1 shared), multi-token prediction, and stability optimizations (zero-centered, weight-decayed layernorm).

- **NVIDIA Nemotron-3 (Super 120B-A12B, Nano 30B-A3B, Ultra 550B-A55B; arXiv 2604.12374, April 14, 2026):** An 88-layer hybrid that "predominantly utilizes Mamba-2 blocks" paired with MoE layers, with "a limited number of self-attention layers strategically inserted as global 'anchors'" for long-range routing; uses NoPE (no positional embeddings), RMSNorm, GQA (32 Q-heads / 2 KV-heads, head dim 128). Introduces **LatentMoE**: experts operate in a compressed latent space (latent size 1024 vs model dim 4096), reducing all-to-all traffic by a factor of d/ℓ, with the savings spent to scale to **512 experts, top-22 routing** — NVIDIA frames this as calling "4× as many expert specialists for the same inference cost." Supports up to 1M-token context. Base-model results: MMLU 86.01, MMLU-Pro 75.65, MATH 84.84, GPQA-Diamond 60.0, RULER-1M 71.0. Headline (inference) efficiency claims: up to 2.2× and 7.5× higher throughput vs GPT-OSS-120B and Qwen3.5-122B respectively.

- **DeepSeek-V4 (V4-Pro 1.6T-A49B; V4-Flash 284B-A13B), April 2026:** Uses a hybrid of **Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA)** (≈4× compression via softmax-gated pooling + an FP4 "lightning indexer" for top-k block selection) and **Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA)** (≈128× compression). At 1M tokens, V4-Pro uses only ~27% of the single-token inference FLOPs and 10% of the KV cache of V3.2 (V4-Flash ~10% FLOPs, ~7% KV cache). Also introduces **Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC)** projecting connection matrices onto a manifold via the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm, controlling signal amplification to 1.6× vs ~3000× for unconstrained hyper-connections — enabling a 4× wider residual stream for only ~6.7% training-time overhead. Pretrained on 32T+ tokens (Flash 32T, Pro 33T) using Muon.

- **State-space model advances — Mamba-3 (ICLR 2026 Oral; Lahoti, Li, Chen, Wang, Bick, Kolter, Dao, Gu — CMU/Princeton/Together/Cartesia; arXiv 2603.15569):** Three improvements over Mamba-2: (1) trapezoidal discretization (replacing Euler's method) for better quality, (2) complex-valued state updates enabling state-tracking (parity, arithmetic) and connecting to data-dependent RoPE, (3) a MIMO (multi-input multi-output) formulation for higher arithmetic intensity without added decode latency. At 1.5B scale, Mamba-3 improves average downstream accuracy by 0.6 points over the next-best model (Gated DeltaNet), with the MIMO variant adding +1.2 more (1.8 total); matches Mamba-2 perplexity at half the state size. **Gated DeltaNet-2** (arXiv 2605.22791, May 2026) decouples erase and write in linear attention.

- **Alternative sparsity scaling — "Scaling Embeddings Outperforms Scaling Experts" (Liu et al., Meituan/Nanbeige, arXiv 2601.21204):** Argues MoE expert scaling faces diminishing returns; proposes scaling **N-gram embeddings** as an orthogonal sparsity dimension. Their LongCat-Flash-Lite (68.5B params, ~3B active, >30B in embeddings) outperforms parameter-equivalent MoE baselines, though the advantage diminishes and is overtaken by MoE once N-gram embeddings exceed ~50% of total parameters; gains are larger in wider (not deeper) models.

- **Mechanistic understanding — "The Spike, the Sparse and the Sink" (Sun, Canziani, LeCun, Zhu, NYU/Meta; arXiv 2603.05498, March 2026):** Shows massive activations and attention sinks are **architectural artifacts of pre-norm design and short-context training**, not functional necessities — massive activations act as "implicit global parameters" via SwiGLU blocks in a near-identity regime (a "Rise–Plateau–Fall" trajectory across layers); ablating pre-norm decouples the two phenomena, and training on long contexts collapses the sink ratio. Implies cleaner architectures could avoid these quantization-breaking outliers.

### B. Optimizers and Training Dynamics

**Muon** (Keller Jordan et al.) orthogonalizes the momentum matrix via Newton-Schulz iteration before the weight update, exploiting the near-low-rank structure of 2D weight gradients; it performs approximately steepest descent under the spectral norm (Bernstein & Newhouse), keeps only one momentum buffer (less memory than Adam's two), and has <1% FLOP overhead.

- **Scaling validation:** Moonshot's "Muon is Scalable for LLM Training" (Liu et al., arXiv 2502.16982; Moonlight, a 3B/16B-parameter MoE on 5.7T tokens) reported that "Muon achieves comparable performance to AdamW trained counterparts while requiring only approximately 52% of the training FLOPs" under compute-optimal settings (~2× computational efficiency).
- **MuonClip (Kimi K2):** Adds **QK-Clip** (per-head rescaling of query/key projection weights, threshold τ=100, applied when max attention logits exceed it) to fix the "exploding attention logits" instability that vanilla Muon exhibits at trillion-parameter scale. Per the Kimi Team report (arXiv 2507.20534), K2 is "a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) large language model with 32 billion activated parameters and 1 trillion total parameters… Based on MuonClip, K2 was pre-trained on 15.5 trillion tokens with zero loss spike." Schedule: first 10T tokens at constant LR 2e-4, then 5.5T with cosine decay to 2e-5; global batch 67M tokens. Layout: 384 experts, top-8, 61 layers, MLA attention, 160K vocab, 128K context.
- **DeepSeek-V4** uses Muon as the main optimizer (AdamW on embeddings, MTP heads, RMSNorm) across 32T+ tokens — a notable departure credited for faster convergence.
- **Essential AI "Practical Efficiency of Muon for Pretraining" (arXiv 2505.02222):** Demonstrates Muon expands the Pareto frontier vs AdamW at large batch sizes and combines with muP for hyperparameter transfer.

**The sobering counter-evidence — "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers and Where to Find Them" (Wen, Hall, Ma, Liang, Stanford; arXiv 2509.02046):** With rigorous, equal hyperparameter tuning, no optimizer achieves the advertised 2× step speedup; the best is ~1.4× over AdamW. Matrix-based optimizers (Muon, SOAP, Kron, Scion) consistently beat scalar ones (1.2–1.4×), but "the speedup of matrix-based optimizers is inversely proportional to model scale, decreasing from 1.4× over AdamW for 0.1B parameter models to merely 1.1× for 1.2B parameter models." The winner is also **regime-dependent**: Muon wins at 1–4× Chinchilla data-to-model ratios but is overtaken by SOAP and Kron at ≥8× (over-trained regimes). This is critical guidance given that frontier models are now massively over-trained.

- **"Towards Robust Scaling Laws for Optimizers" (Volkova, Safaryan, Lampert, Alistarh; arXiv 2602.07712, Feb 2026):** Shows separate Chinchilla-style laws per optimizer are ill-conditioned; proposes a law with shared power-law exponents and optimizer-specific rescaling factors for direct comparison.
- **Newton–Muon (arXiv 2604.01472, 2026)** and **8-bit Muon** (ICLR 2026, ~74% optimizer-memory reduction via blockwise quantization) are active refinements.

**Stability techniques** are a recurring theme: QK-Clip/QK-Norm, zero-centered weight-decayed layernorm (Qwen3-Next), manifold-constrained connections (DeepSeek mHC), and Warmup-Stable-Decay (WSD) schedules (Nemotron-3 uses WSD with 200B-token warmup to peak LR 4.5e-4, then minus-sqrt decay over the final 5T tokens to 4.5e-6).

### C. Low-Precision Pre-Training (the year's biggest efficiency lever)

The frontier moved from FP8 (now mainstream on Hopper/Blackwell) to **4-bit (FP4) pre-training**:

- **NVFP4** (NVIDIA): E2M1 elements in 16-element micro-blocks with E4M3 micro-block scales and an FP32 global scale. Key techniques for stability: **Random Hadamard Transforms** to spread outliers, **2D block scaling** for weights, **stochastic rounding** on gradients, and selective BF16 layers. The "Pretraining LLMs with NVFP4" paper (NVIDIA, arXiv 2509.25149) trained a 12B hybrid Mamba-Transformer on a trillion-token scale matching FP8 across downstream tasks; NVIDIA reports throughput gains up to **1.59× for NVFP4 vs BF16** with comparable accuracy on Blackwell B200.
- **Production proof at scale — Nemotron-3 Super:** Per the technical report, "Nemotron 3 Super showcases large-scale stable training in NVFP4 up to 25T tokens" (20T diversity phase + 5T high-quality phase). The recipe keeps the **final 15% of the network, latent projections, MTP layers, QKV/attention projections, and embeddings in BF16**, and the Mamba output projection in MXFP8 (to avoid underflow). A notable finding: NVFP4 produces ~3× more zero-valued weight gradients than BF16 at the same token horizon (underflow of sub-1e-12 gradients), with "zero-valued weight gradient elements account[ing] for 7% of total parameters" by end of training; switching to MXFP8 near the end improved the loss trajectory but yielded **no downstream accuracy gain**, so the full NVFP4 recipe was kept.
- **Quartet / Quartet II (Alistarh group, IST Austria; arXiv 2601.22813):** "Native FP4 training can be optimal for LLMs," focusing on improved unbiased gradient estimation; the field has converged on two principles — minimize forward-pass MSE (representation capacity) and keep backward-pass gradient estimators unbiased (since biased gradients accumulate systematic error over long runs).
- Hardware: NVIDIA Blackwell (B200/GB200/GB300) provides native FP4 FLOPs; Google's Ironwood TPU implements native FP8 (E4M3 forward, E5M2 backward), with Anthropic committing to 1M+ chips in 2026.

### D. Pre-Training Objectives

- **Multi-Token Prediction (MTP):** Now near-standard. Originated with Meta's "Better & Faster LLMs via Multi-token Prediction" (Gloeckle et al., 2024); used in DeepSeek-V3/V4, Qwen3-Next, and Nemotron-3 (2 shared-weight MTP layers, loss scale 0.3). Primary payoff is **native speculative decoding** — Nemotron-3 reports an average acceptance length of 3.45 tokens (vs DeepSeek-R1's 2.70 and Qwen3-Next's 3.33 on SPEED-Bench) — plus modest validation-loss improvement.
- **Future Summary Prediction (FSP)** (Mahajan et al., ICLR 2026; arXiv 2510.14751): Argues MTP captures only short-range dependencies; instead trains an auxiliary head to predict a compact representation of the long-term future (a bag-of-words summary, or an embedding from a right-to-left reverse LM). 3B and 8B pre-training experiments show gains over both NTP and MTP on math, reasoning, and coding.
- **Diffusion Language Models (the most radical bet):** "Diffusion Language Models are Super Data Learners" (Ni et al., arXiv 2511.03276) reports a **"crossover"**: under data constraints, DLMs surpass autoregressive models by training more epochs, attributed to any-order modeling, super-dense bidirectional compute, and built-in Monte Carlo augmentation. Concretely, "a 1B-parameter DLM achieves >56% accuracy on HellaSwag and >33% on MMLU using only 1B tokens… just by repeating standard pre-training data" — specifically 480 epochs on 1B unique tokens (480B total), vs AR baselines' 41% (HellaSwag) and 29% (MMLU); and "a 1.7B DLM trained with a ~1.5T-token compute budget on 10B unique Python tokens overtakes an AR coder" under matched settings. This is the strongest argument for diffusion as the web's unique-token supply plateaus. Caveats: perplexity isn't directly comparable (variational bound), inference is less mature, and memorization/contamination risk rises with heavy data reuse. LLaDA (8B, competitive with LLaMA3 8B) remains the largest from-scratch diffusion LM; block-diffusion code models (Stable-DiffCoder, Jan 2026) and byte-level BLT-Diffusion are active areas.

### E. Tokenization and Vocabulary

- **Tokenizer-free / byte-level models** continue to mature. Meta's Byte Latent Transformer (BLT) dynamically groups bytes into entropy-based patches; "Compute Optimal Tokenization" (arXiv 2605.01188) trained 988 BLT models (50M–7B) to study how byte-per-token compression rate interacts with scaling. New 2026 work focuses on **distilling** token-based models into byte-level ones ("Distilling Token-Trained Models into Byte-Level Models," arXiv 2602.01007, Feb 2026, which distills a standard transformer into an H-Net-style hierarchical byte model; ByteFlow, arXiv 2603.03583, learns its own segmentation via coding-rate compression). Byte models promise robustness to noise, better multilingual equity, and orthographic knowledge, but full from-scratch byte pre-training remains compute-prohibitive — hence the distillation focus. H-Net (Hwang et al., end-to-end hierarchical chunking) is a key reference.
- Larger vocabularies are standard (Kimi K2: 160K). N-gram embeddings (LongCat) blur the tokenization/embedding boundary.

### F. Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training

The field is in a "Chinchilla successors" phase, refining the original L = E + A/N^α + B/D^β for new regimes:

- **The data-constrained regime is now central** because compute is growing faster than unique data. Muennighoff et al.'s data-constrained law (up to ~4 epochs ≈ as valuable as fresh data) is the baseline. "Practical Scaling Laws: Converting Compute into Performance in a Data-Constrained World" (Bryant et al., arXiv 2605.09189, May 2026) extends Chinchilla with explicit undercapacity, undertraining, and overfitting terms (L = E + (L₀−E)·h/(1+h)), fitting validation-loss *minima* (not final), which is critical in the N≫D overfitting regime. "Prescriptive Scaling Laws for Data Constrained Training" (arXiv 2605.01640) finds strong weight decay (λ=1.0) reduces the overfitting penalty by ~70%, and that beyond a compute threshold, adding capacity beats more epochs. "Scaling Laws for Mixture Pretraining Under Data Constraints" (arXiv 2605.12715) jointly optimizes mixture weights and repetition for constrained-vs-abundant domains.
- **Optimizer-aware and architecture-aware laws:** "Scaling Laws Meet Model Architecture: Toward Inference-Efficient LLMs" (arXiv 2510.18245) reports conditionally-optimal architectures achieving up to 2.1% higher accuracy and 42% greater inference throughput than LLaMA-3.2. "Holistic Scaling Laws for Optimal Mixture-of-Experts Architecture Optimization" (arXiv 2603.21862) maps any compute budget to a complete MoE configuration via a joint FLOPs/active-params/total-params constraint triad. "Scaling Laws for Optimal Data Mixtures" (arXiv 2507.09404) predicts target-domain loss from mixture coefficients with only a few small runs.
- **MoE compute-optimality:** Models like DeepSeek-V3/V4 are heavily over-trained on the active-parameter view; the total-vs-active parameter ambiguity means "20 tokens/param" Chinchilla heuristics need restating for sparse models.

### G. Data Strategies

The defining 2026 shift is **quality and synthesis over raw quantity**:

- **Quality filtering:** FineWeb/FineWeb-Edu (classifier-driven educational filtering; FineWeb is a 15T-token corpus, FineWeb-Edu a 1.3T educational subset) remain the open baselines; Ultra-FineWeb and Nemotron-CC refine the verification. Nemotron-CC's finding that long-horizon training (≥15T tokens) must balance unique content with quality (not just aggressive filtering) is influential. FineWeb2 extends the pipeline multilingually.
- **Synthetic pre-training data is now standard:** Rephrasing/rewriting pipelines (WRAP) and large-scale synthetic generation. "How Can We Synthesize High-Quality Pretraining Data?" (arXiv 2604.13977) generated >1 trillion synthetic tokens and found structured formats (tables, math problems, FAQs, tutorials) consistently outperform curated web baselines and prior synthetic methods. Nemotron-3 released Nemotron-Pretraining-Specialized-v1.1 (synthetic code concepts, ~3.5M MMLU-style MCQs ≈1.6B tokens, formal logic, economics); its MMLU-style synthetic data improved MMLU (77.22→77.51), MATH-Level-5 (78.55→79.05), and AIME-2024 (53.3→56.7) in continued-pretraining ablations. Kimi K2 attributes part of its gains to rephrasing high-quality corpora into "learning-notes" formats.
- **Two-phase / curriculum / annealing:** Nemotron-3 uses an 80% diversity phase (20T tokens) followed by a 20% high-quality phase (5T tokens). DeepSeek-V4 trains separate domain specialists then merges ten teacher models via distillation.
- **Dynamic / online data curation:** "Rethinking Data Curation in LLM Training: Online Reweighting Offers Better Generalization than Offline Methods" (Zhao et al., arXiv 2605.05227, April 2026) proposes ADAPT, reframing selection/mixing/balancing as a unified **online reweighting** problem with adaptive per-sample learning rates, reporting better generalization than offline filtering (which alters dataset size and harms diversity). Data Mixing Agent (arXiv 2507.15640) learns to reweight domains via RL for continual pre-training.
- **Checkpoint merging during pre-training:** Nemotron-3 merges weights in sliding windows (125B/250B/500B tokens) during the WSD stable phase for a 2–4 point benchmark gain and estimated ~16% FLOP savings.

### H. Long-Context Pre-Training and Efficiency Systems

- **Native sparse attention** trained end-to-end: DeepSeek's NSA (arXiv 2502.11089) and the V3.2/V4 sparse-attention lineage are designed to be trainable from pretraining, not bolted on. DeepSeek-V4 ramps sequence length 4K→16K with dense attention, then switches on the sparse path at a 64K stage, with an FP4 indexer quantized FP32→BF16 for 2× speedup at 99.7% top-k recall.
- **Parallelism:** Composable 4D parallelism (FSDP + pipeline + context + expert parallel) is standard for 1T-scale models at 256K context. NVIDIA's Hybrid Expert Parallel reports up to 514% throughput improvement for MoE expert-parallel communication. Nemotron-3's long-context extension uses 64-way context parallel, 2-way tensor parallel, and 64-way expert parallel on GB200 GPUs to train at 1M context.

## Recommendations

**For a team planning a new frontier pre-training run (staged):**

1. **Architecture (do now):** Default to a **hybrid linear-attention/Mamba + MoE transformer**, not a dense transformer. Use a 3:1–4:1 ratio of linear (Gated DeltaNet or Mamba-2) to full-attention layers, with attention layers as periodic global anchors. This is the single highest-confidence best practice, validated across Qwen, Nemotron, DeepSeek, and MiniMax. Adopt MTP heads from the start for near-free speculative decoding.

2. **Precision (high-value, moderate risk):** Pilot **NVFP4 pre-training** on Blackwell-class hardware, keeping embeddings, the final ~15% of layers, attention projections, and MTP heads in BF16, and using Random Hadamard Transforms + stochastic rounding (and MXFP8 for underflow-prone layers like Mamba output projections). Benchmark threshold: if NVFP4 matches a BF16 control run's downstream accuracy within noise on a 1T-token ablation (as Nemotron-3/NVIDIA report), commit to it for the full run — the ~1.6× throughput plus memory gains are decisive. If you see persistent loss-curve divergence or downstream degradation, fall back to FP8/MXFP8.

3. **Optimizer (validate, don't assume):** Use **Muon/MuonClip** for matrix parameters (AdamW for embeddings/norms/heads), adding QK-Clip if you observe attention-logit explosion. **But** always run your own fairly-tuned AdamW baseline — per "Fantastic Pretraining Optimizers," expect only ~1.1–1.4× speedup at your scale (and closer to 1.1× above ~1B params), and if you are training at ≥8× Chinchilla ratio, evaluate SOAP/Kron, which overtake Muon there.

4. **Data (decisive for quality):** Invest disproportionately in (a) classifier-based quality filtering, (b) large-scale synthetic data with structured formats, and (c) a two-phase curriculum (diversity → high-quality anneal). Consider online reweighting (ADAPT-style) over brittle offline filtering. Benchmark threshold: track whether added synthetic data improves held-out MMLU/MATH/code in 100B-token continued-pretraining ablations before committing trillions of tokens. Use checkpoint merging in the stable LR phase for "free" gains.

5. **Experimental bets (R&D track, parallel):** Allocate a research budget to **diffusion LMs** if you are data-constrained (the "super data learner" crossover is the most credible path to a step-change in data efficiency), and to **new objectives** (FSP) for long-horizon reasoning. Treat these as 6–18 month bets, not the main run.

**Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:** A fairly-tuned optimizer demonstrating a sustained >1.5× speedup at >10B scale; a diffusion LM matching AR quality at equal *compute* (not just equal data) at >8B scale; or FP4 training showing downstream degradation at >10T tokens would each shift the calculus.

## Synthesis: Which directions are most likely to drive a "big leap"?

Ranking the experimental frontiers by expected impact and evidence quality:

1. **Native 4-bit (NVFP4) pre-training — highest near-term certainty.** It is already in production at 25T tokens (Nemotron-3) with matched downstream accuracy and ~1.6× throughput; this is a direct, compounding compute/cost multiplier on every future run. Lowest-risk "leap."
2. **Diffusion LMs as super data learners — highest upside, higher risk.** If the data-constrained crossover holds at 8B+ scale and equal compute, it would fundamentally relax the data wall that bounds autoregressive scaling. The 1.7B-vs-AR-coder result is the most striking single datapoint of the half-year, but inference maturity and apples-to-apples compute comparisons remain unresolved.
3. **Hybrid sub-quadratic architectures (Mamba-3 / Gated DeltaNet-2 + sparse attention) — already winning.** These make million-token-context pre-training and inference economical, which in turn unlocks agentic training regimes. The leap here is less about loss-per-FLOP and more about enabling capabilities (long-horizon agentic reasoning) that quadratic attention prices out.
4. **New objectives (MTP → FSP) — incremental but real.** MTP's speculative-decoding payoff is banked; FSP's long-horizon signal is promising but unproven beyond 8B.
5. **Optimizer innovation — diminishing returns at frontier scale.** Muon/MuonClip's stability contribution (zero-spike trillion-token runs) is genuinely valuable, but the rigorous evidence says raw speedup converges toward ~1.1× as models grow, so this is a refinement, not a leap.

The combinatorial bet most likely to define late-2026 frontier models: **a hybrid Mamba/attention MoE, pre-trained natively in FP4 with Muon-class optimizers on a heavily synthetic, dynamically-curated 25–40T-token corpus with MTP heads** — essentially the Nemotron-3/DeepSeek-V4/Kimi-K2 convergence, with diffusion pre-training as the wildcard that could reset the data-efficiency frontier entirely.

## Caveats
- **Scale-extrapolation risk is the dominant caveat.** Many exciting results (Mamba-3 at 1.5B, FSP at 3–8B, optimizer comparisons at ≤1.2B) are at small scale; the "Fantastic Optimizers" finding that gains shrink with scale should be the default prior for any method validated only below ~2B parameters.
- **arXiv date encoding:** 2026 papers use the YYMM.NNNNN convention (2601 = Jan 2026, 2605 = May 2026). Primary technical reports (Nemotron-3, Kimi K2, DeepSeek-V4) are prioritized over secondary coverage.
- **Some model specifics rely on secondary coverage.** DeepSeek-V4's CSA/HCA/mHC details come substantially from independent reviewers and relayed technical-report content; and Nemotron-3's report describes its Mamba-to-attention layer split qualitatively ("predominantly Mamba-2," "a limited number of self-attention anchors") rather than as an explicit numeric ratio — do not cite a precise ratio for it.
- **Optimizer hype vs. reality:** Vendor and enthusiast claims of "2×" optimizer speedups do not survive rigorous equal-tuning; the realistic figure is ~1.1–1.4× and regime-dependent. Likewise, the often-repeated "FP8 gives ~30–40% throughput" claim could not be tied to a single authoritative named source and should be treated cautiously; the verifiable Blackwell figure is NVFP4's ~1.59× over BF16.
- **Diffusion LM comparisons are confounded:** perplexity is not directly comparable to AR models (variational bound), inference stacks are immature, and heavy data reuse raises memorization/contamination concerns.
- **Pre-training vs post-training boundary:** Per scope, this report excludes SFT/RLHF/DPO/GRPO; some cited reports (Kimi K2, Nemotron-3, DeepSeek-V4) include post-training stages that have been deliberately omitted.
